# Dave McKean — material, association, density, image-text relation

Use this dossier when the visual medium, material register, density, or relationship between text and image must carry meaning. Do not reduce McKean to collage, texture, masks, or a mixed-media look.

Read evidence anchors `M1–M4` in [master-evidence-ledger.md](master-evidence-ledger.md). They ground the operations below; they are not copy for the artifact.

## Research findings

### Material follows story need — P

In his official FAQ, McKean says he chooses materials according to the job: direct, close storytelling may need pen, brush, or pencil; a more symbolic approach may need collage, paint, or digital work; emotion and atmosphere decide.

**Operational inference — S:** specify what a material or register changes in the narrative condition. Texture without a job is decoration.

### Different narrative territories can use different registers — P

Discussing *Raptor*, McKean describes one narrative in ink, another in pencil, and painted/collage passages for dreams or visions. The choices distinguish worlds and emotional conditions. He also says all of the drawing began in physical ink, pencil, or paint and was then shaped digitally to preserve handmade energy.

**Operational inference — S:** vary register when reality status, viewpoint, emotion, or narrative layer changes. Preserve a unifying grammar so variation does not become drift.

### Simple and complex are conditional — P

McKean explicitly notes that sometimes a simple line does more than overdrawing and sometimes complexity works.

**Operational inference — S:** treat information density as a variable. Test whether the moment needs reduction, accumulation, or a change between them.

### Image must be structurally necessary — P

In *The Comics Journal*, McKean criticizes comics where prose is complete by itself and pictures merely follow it. He argues that story should be carried primarily or at least equally by images, including expression and body language.

**Operational inference — S:** use an `A + B → C` test. Text contributes A, image contributes B, and their relation creates C. If either layer only repeats the other, redesign.

### Improvisation can develop ideas — P

In a *Raptor* interview, McKean describes adopting an improvisational, exploratory development process and discarding/redrawing early scenes. He also describes a project becoming plausible when fragments acquire a strong ending, a beginning, a middle turn, a felt sound, and a reason the work matters.

**Operational inference — S:** generate associations and prototypes, observe what relations emerge, then edit. Improvisation is a search method, not permission to keep accidents.

### Overlap can invite construction without permanent confusion — P

McKean describes overlapping narratives that comment on one another, aiming for temporary intrigue but eventual enough clarity for the reader to work out their relation.

**Operational inference — S:** leave a relation for the audience to construct, but provide constraints and a later integration point.

### Reality and human intention matter — P

McKean emphasizes observation, physical making, and human intention. In *The Comics Journal* he explicitly rejects generative AI as a replacement for the embodied chain from seeing the world to making a mark.

**Operational inference — S:** ground visual choices in observed material and deliberate editing. Do not let generated texture substitute for understanding.

**Disagreement boundary:** this skill may use generative tools because the user requested an AI production workflow. It does not pretend McKean endorses that use. His position is retained as an adversarial question: what observation, intention, and human decision does this generated element carry, and who is responsible for it?

### Incomplete information can activate the reader — P

McKean says comics imply motion and sound rather than supplying them, and that a good comic invites the reader to participate. In *Raptor*, he aimed for local uncertainty while preserving enough later clarity that the parallel narratives could be worked out.

**Operational inference — S:** remove information only when the medium lets the audience construct it from available evidence. Confusion without a recoverable relation is a failure, not participation.

## Image-text responsibility matrix

Use this on every load-bearing frame.

| Field | Required answer |
|---|---|
| `text_job` | What fact, voice, time, or abstraction is most efficient in words? |
| `image_job` | What relation, action, atmosphere, or evidence must be seen? |
| `third_meaning` | What becomes available only through their combination? |
| `text_only_loss` | What essential meaning disappears if the image is removed? |
| `image_only_loss` | What essential meaning disappears if the text is removed? |
| `redundancy_job` | If they repeat, does repetition aid memory, accessibility, rhythm, or emphasis? |

## Register map

| Field | Required answer |
|---|---|
| `territory` | Which reality, viewpoint, time, or emotional condition is this? |
| `material/register` | What line, texture, image mode, or density carries it? |
| `narrative_job` | What changes because this register is used? |
| `continuity_token` | What keeps identity coherent across register changes? |
| `observation_basis` | Which real object, body, place, process, or source grounds it? |
| `simpler_test` | Would a simpler line or cleaner composition do more? |

## STORY protocol

1. Harvest fragments from the source, physical processes, spatial relations, and possible materials.
2. Keep fragments free of fixed symbolic meanings at first.
3. Pair or juxtapose non-obvious fragments and state the relation that emerges.
4. Reject pairings whose meaning exists only in an explanatory paragraph.
5. Build routes with different density patterns and degrees of reader construction.
6. Give the route a beginning, turn, ending, and reason to exist before treating improvisation as structure.
7. Choose a register because it fits the narrative/emotional condition, not because it looks artisanal.
8. Test temporary ambiguity against eventual integration.

## FRAME STUDIO protocol

- Complete the image-text responsibility matrix before layout.
- Complete a register map when material or density changes across the sequence.
- Prototype at least a simple and a layered register when density is uncertain.
- State what every material behavior contributes: line, tear, transparency, weight, overlap, erasure, grain, or collage boundary.
- Use register changes to mark real changes, not to refresh attention mechanically.
- Inspect whether generated material looks physically coherent, grounded, and intentionally selected; surface polish is not evidence of any of those conditions.
- Remove texture, fragments, or layers that do not alter meaning, atmosphere, or pacing.

## Candidate questions

- Why this material for this moment?
- What does the image say that the text cannot?
- What third meaning appears only when these elements meet?
- Does the moment need a simple line or accumulated complexity?
- What relation should the audience construct?
- Where does ambiguity integrate, and how much remains open?

## Failure conditions

- Collage or texture functions as instant “artfulness.”
- Material symbols use a universal dictionary rather than context.
- Image illustrates the sentence without adding information.
- Every frame has the same density.
- Register changes are unmotivated or identity continuity is lost.
- AI-generated surface polish replaces observation and intention.
- The skill invokes McKean to legitimize AI output while hiding his stated opposition to generative AI.
- Improvisation produces fragments but no turn, ending, or reason for the sequence to exist.

## When to abstain or stay secondary

Stay secondary when exact procedural exposition or a clean causal comparison would be obscured by material association.
