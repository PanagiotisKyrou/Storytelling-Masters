# Takehiko Inoue — character life, bodily truth, timing, revision

Use this dossier when the work needs lived human presence, physical credibility, natural timing, changed perspective, or fine-grained correction after seeing the frame. Do not reduce Inoue to manga linework, brush texture, or exaggerated sports motion.

## Source caveat

The strongest evidence comes from Inoue's official statement and *The First Slam Dunk* press kit. Translated interviews about *Vagabond* are useful but lower-confidence; keep their claims bounded.

## Research findings

### Characters remain alive beyond the plot — P

In an official 2022 statement, Inoue says the *Slam Dunk* characters continued to feel alive to him, and that aging revealed aspects and perspectives he could not see or illustrate earlier.

**Operational inference — S:** treat a character as more than a message function. Ask what they notice, want, know, and do outside the convenience of the current thesis.

### Changed creator perspective can produce a new story — P

Inoue describes the film as emerging from perspectives accumulated over decades, including pain and limits he understood differently from his twenties.

**Operational inference — S:** the same source event can yield a different story when the current point of view changes. Name that perspective rather than pretending the source itself changed.

### Story can start from rough pillars — P

In the press-kit interview, Inoue says he began the film by listing what he wanted to do and placing rough pillars.

**Operational inference — S:** begin with load-bearing moments, then discover the exact path. Do not require a fully solved outline before visual exploration.

### “Life” is a frame-level criterion — P

Inoue repeatedly describes his retouching goal as giving characters life. He personally corrected many cuts and used drawn instructions for expression and timing.

**Operational inference — S:** inspect rendered characters at the level of gaze, posture, weight, expression, and timing. When the feeling is wrong, redraw or retime; do not defend the prompt.

### Naturalism can be more intense than exaggeration — P

Inoue says the film should not be overly exaggerated and should feel like ordinary high-school students on a basketball court.

**Operational inference — S:** test restrained, heightened, and observational versions. Choose the one that creates credibility and force, not the loudest reaction.

### Texture must support life — P

He sought a textured, paper-like image rather than a smooth/shiny CG appearance, while still using CG to coordinate ten separately moving players.

**Operational inference — S:** combine tools according to the movement/production problem while protecting the human and material qualities the work needs.

### Repeated pilots exposed a qualitative failure — C/P

Producer Toshiyuki Matsui describes earlier pilots being rejected because the characters did not feel alive. Later work combined 3D and 2D approaches and continued through repeated proposals and trials.

**Operational inference — S:** prototype the hardest qualitative claim early. A technically impressive render can still fail the primary life/feeling criterion.

### Embodied understanding supports speed — P through translated interview

In a translated interview, Inoue links convincing speed to understanding body mechanics, axes, and center of gravity.

**Operational inference — S:** motion credibility comes from weight transfer and body logic, not speed lines alone.

### Moment/person may outrank plot machinery — P through translated interview

In a translated *Vagabond* interview, Inoue says his interest is closer to understanding Musashi as a person and to poetry than conventional story delivery.

**Operational inference — S:** allow precise moments to accumulate meaning. Use this as a counterweight to manufactured drama, not as a universal rejection of structure.

## STORY protocol

1. Identify the people, objects, or moments that must feel alive.
2. Write what each person knows, wants, notices, and can physically do.
3. Place rough pillars from the most truthful moments.
4. Generate small actions or reactions that reveal character without invented melodrama.
5. Compare restrained, heightened, and observational versions.
6. State how the current creator/audience perspective changes significance.
7. Preserve a precise human or physical residue at the ending.

## FRAME STUDIO protocol

- Check center of gravity, weight transfer, gaze, hands, recovery, and reaction timing.
- Avoid simultaneous generic reactions from every person.
- Prototype the hardest “feeling of life” frame early.
- Use drawn or visual correction notes for expression and timing, not vague adjectives.
- Retouch/rebuild the actual frame until the character or object feels present.
- Keep texture, tool choice, and compositing subordinate to life and action.

## Candidate questions

- Does this character exist beyond the message?
- What tiny physical detail proves the world understands this action?
- What is the body's axis and center of gravity?
- Would a restrained reaction feel more truthful?
- Which perspective became visible only with time?
- What exactly should be redrawn after seeing the render?

## Failure conditions

- “Human” means generic smiling, shock, or sadness.
- Bodies, hands, gaze, weight, or timing are physically false.
- Drama is added because the source feels quiet.
- Texture is treated as life.
- A polished render survives despite lifeless characters.
- Translated interview claims are presented as universal Inoue doctrine.

## When to abstain or stay secondary

Stay secondary when the task is a purely abstract diagram, a dense factual table, or a non-human system where embodied micro-truth adds no value.
