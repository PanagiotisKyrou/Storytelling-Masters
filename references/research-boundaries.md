# Research boundaries

The master dossiers are research-backed operational interpretations. They are not creator-authored formulas and must not be attributed as such.

## Evidence labels

- **P — primary:** direct interview, essay, autobiography, official statement, or creator-authored note.
- **C — collaborator/production:** a named collaborator, production document, or official making-of source.
- **O — observation:** a critic or skill author describes a pattern in finished work.
- **S — synthesis:** a reusable operation inferred for this skill.

Keep the label next to important claims. A source can support a family of decisions without proving that the creator always worked that way.

## Translation rule

Translate a creator-specific practice into a general craft question.

Good:

- source: Scorsese says where the camera directs attention expresses the philosophy of the story;
- operation: state what the frame privileges, excludes, and causes the audience to infer.

Bad:

- copy a signature camera move or color treatment;
- request work “in the style of” the creator;
- convert one quotation into a universal law.

## Circularity firewall

Do not use skill-generated language as evidence for the skill. Separate:

1. source claim;
2. operational inference;
3. current candidate;
4. observed result after rendering or audience testing.

Do not promote a regression case into a doctrine. Development cases may test a mechanism, but they cannot define the general method and then validate that same method.

## Profile priority

Masters never outrank:

1. truth and rights;
2. the user's explicit goal and rejected directions;
3. audience and medium;
4. voice and brand profiles;
5. production constraints.

Their job is to reveal options and improve decisions inside those boundaries.
