# Research boundaries

The master dossiers are research-backed operational interpretations. They are not creator-authored formulas and must not be attributed as such.

## Evidence labels

- **P — primary:** direct interview, essay, autobiography, official statement, or creator-authored note.
- **P-I — primary through intermediary:** the creator's words reproduced by a named publisher, translator, archive, or independent host when the original is unavailable.
- **C — collaborator/production:** a named collaborator, production document, or official making-of source.
- **O — observation:** a critic or skill author describes a pattern in finished work.
- **S — synthesis:** a reusable operation inferred for this skill.

Keep the label next to important claims. A source can support a family of decisions without proving that the creator always worked that way.

## Quote integrity

Use [master-evidence-ledger.md](master-evidence-ledger.md) for short verified anchors and source limits.

- Preserve exact wording only inside quotation marks; otherwise paraphrase.
- Record speaker, context, date when available, source class, translation/hosting caveat, and the bounded claim the passage supports.
- Do not use quote aggregators, unsourced image cards, fan captions, or repeated web aphorisms as evidence.
- If a quotation cannot be traced beyond a secondary page, label it `P-I` or exclude it from the core protocol.
- Keep quotations in internal reasoning by default. Do not name-drop a master or insert their words into the user's artifact unless the user requests it and the quote serves the story.

## Contradiction rule

Creators can change methods, use different methods in different media, or hold positions the skill does not fully adopt. Preserve the conflict instead of flattening it.

Example: McKean explicitly rejects generative AI as a substitute for observation and human material practice, while this skill may operate inside an AI production workflow. Retain that disagreement as an adversarial test; do not imply endorsement.

## Translation rule

Translate a creator-specific practice into a general craft question.

Good:

- source: Scorsese says where the camera directs attention expresses the philosophy of the story;
- operation: state what the frame privileges, excludes, and causes the audience to infer.

Bad:

- copy a signature camera move or color treatment;
- request work “in the style of” the creator;
- convert one quotation into a universal law.

## Evidence-to-operation test

Before adding a creator-derived operation, answer:

1. What did the source literally establish?
2. What context or medium bounded it?
3. What part is this skill's inference?
4. What present decision will the inference improve?
5. What failure would show that the translation was wrong for this brief?

If questions 3–5 cannot be answered, keep the passage in the evidence ledger but do not turn it into a protocol.

## Circularity firewall

Do not use skill-generated language as evidence for the skill. Separate:

1. source claim;
2. operational inference;
3. current candidate;
4. observed result after rendering or audience testing.

Do not promote a regression case into a doctrine. Development cases may test a mechanism, but they cannot define the general method and then validate that same method.

## Profile priority

Masters never outrank:

1. truth and rights;
2. the user's explicit goal and rejected directions;
3. audience and medium;
4. voice and brand profiles;
5. production constraints.

Their job is to reveal options and improve decisions inside those boundaries.
