# FRAME STUDIO mode

Create and improve the actual visual artifact. A frame specification, prompt pack, or critique is an intermediate state.

## 1. Establish the sequence grammar

From the story beats, define:

- invariant visual anchors, if any;
- elements allowed to transform;
- viewpoint and spatial logic;
- density rhythm;
- palette and type roles;
- recurring motifs and the delta at each return;
- transition logic;
- what must remain legible without explanatory copy.

Do not make every frame share the same poster layout merely for consistency. Consistency is a controlled grammar, not cloned composition.

## 2. Frame ledger

For every frame or slide record:

- ID and narrative beat;
- audience model before and after;
- one primary story job;
- focal hierarchy and eye path;
- visual event or relation;
- composition: scale, position, depth, balance/tension, negative space;
- imagery/material behavior;
- typography and exact text responsibility;
- image responsibility and text-image delta;
- continuity anchor and change from previous frame;
- dimensions, crop, safe zones, contrast, and accessibility;
- build method and source rights;
- known risks and forbidden shortcuts.

One primary job does not mean one object or one idea. A complex frame may coordinate several elements when they serve one coherent audience change.

## 3. Master-directed frame work

Use the scouts, then deepen only useful dossiers.

- Lynch can create clue, breach, recurrence, or unresolved visual residue.
- Scorsese can alter what the frame privileges, excludes, and reveals through order.
- McKean can choose material/register/density and enforce image-text interdependence.
- Kurosawa can organize action, forces, environment, tempo, and editorial cuts.
- Inoue can correct bodily truth, expression, timing, and the feeling of life.

Do not copy their recognizable aesthetics.

## 4. Build

Use the most appropriate available creation path:

- presentation or slide tooling for editable decks;
- image generation/editing for raster visual layers;
- vector or code-native construction for exact diagrams and repeatable layouts;
- existing design tooling when the user supplies a native design.

Keep exact typography and diagrams editable when possible. Separate generated imagery from typesetting if the image tool cannot render text reliably.

Create the artifact at its intended aspect ratio and output dimensions. For sequences, create a contact sheet or thumbnail overview in addition to individual renders.

## 5. Pixel inspection

Inspect each rendered frame, not only its source instructions.

### Per-frame trial

- Is the intended focal point actually first?
- Is text readable at the real viewing size?
- Are line breaks, hierarchy, spacing, and alignment intentional?
- Does the composition feel balanced, tense, quiet, dense, or unstable for a reason?
- Does color guide meaning and remain accessible?
- Are imagery, anatomy, perspective, identity, materials, and lighting coherent?
- Does the frame add story information or felt experience?
- Does it accidentally resemble a stock template, unrelated prior project, or prompt artifact?
- Are brand and voice visible in decisions rather than pasted on as decoration?

### Sequence trial

- Does the order create a causal, perceptual, emotional, or argumentative progression?
- Is there meaningful variation in scale, density, viewpoint, and tempo?
- Can the audience track what persists and what changes?
- Does recurrence return with a delta?
- Are any frames filler, duplicate explanation, or beautiful dead ends?
- Do the first and last frames create a specific change when compared?

## 6. Correct and rebuild

Classify findings:

- **local correction:** spacing, crop, line break, contrast, alignment;
- **frame rebuild:** wrong focal event, weak composition, generic image, false gesture;
- **sequence backtrack:** story beat/order/direction cannot carry the intended change.

Apply corrections at the right level. Do not use cosmetic polish to hide a story failure.

Re-render after material changes and verify the corrected pixels.

## 7. Definition of done

FRAME STUDIO is complete only when:

- actual frames were created or rebuilt;
- intended output dimensions were rendered;
- each frame and the whole sequence were visually inspected;
- material failures were corrected and re-rendered;
- remaining uncertainty or blocked verification is explicit;
- editable/source and delivery formats meet the brief.

If any item is absent, name the result accurately: concept, production specification, draft, or unverified export.
