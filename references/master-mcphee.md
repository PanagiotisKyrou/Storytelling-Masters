# John McPhee — nonfiction evidence, selection, structure, orientation

Use this dossier when truthful material is abundant but the work has no convincing organizing relation, when chronology and theme compete, when research gaps appear during drafting, or when references and omissions make the reader lose orientation. Do not reduce McPhee to outlines, index cards, long-form journalism, or a single formula.

Read evidence anchors `J1–J8` in [master-evidence-ledger.md](master-evidence-ledger.md). They ground the operations below; they are not copy for the artifact.

## Source-backed research

### Every subject starts at square one

In “Structure,” McPhee describes each new project as a fresh structural problem. The useful transfer is not a reusable outline shape but the demand to discover the relation that belongs to the gathered material. In *The Pine Barrens*, one person became the connective route through otherwise miscellaneous topics; another subject may require chronology, counterpoint, place, encounter, or something else.

### Fact constrains form without eliminating choice

McPhee compares factual material to groceries already placed on the counter: the writer can arrange what is there but cannot rename one ingredient as another. He also says structure partly dictates itself and partly leaves room for interesting choices. This is a direct guard against both fabrication and the opposite mistake—assuming that facts arrive with one inevitable order.

### Anchored and movable material behave differently

For *Encounters with the Archdruid*, McPhee coded the notes, separated components, and distinguished journey-bound material from biographical or thematic material that could move. The relation between an anchored event and a movable section gave him a stable order. The transferable operation is classification by structural freedom, not imitation of cards or software.

### Selection begins during reporting

In “Elicitation,” McPhee calls writing and note-taking acts of selection. He records openly, warns against relying on memory, and distinguishes representative editing from invention. He also describes research continuing during composition because drafting exposes what the writer does not know.

### Reader orientation must not depend on borrowed knowledge

In “Frame of Reference,” McPhee argues that a reference fails when the reader must already know the person, work, or event that is supposed to provide the description. A reference may still work when the writer repays the borrowed vividness with enough concrete context.

### Endings can be discovered upstream

McPhee advises looking back when a planned ending does not work; the piece may already have ended. He describes completion as the point where he cannot presently do better, while acknowledging that someone else might. This is evidence for a personal stopping judgment, not a universal quality certificate.

## Operational inference — S

McPhee contributes a **source-architecture loop**. First make the evidence legible, then discover a relation that can carry it. Drafting is allowed to expose missing research, but unsupported material stays visibly unresolved. Omission is a structural choice that must preserve fairness and causality.

This loop belongs before stylistic narrative development only when the source architecture is actually open. It should abstain from fiction, a settled evidence structure, or a local sentence/frame problem.

## Observable decision model

| Dimension | Source-backed pattern | Operational translation |
|---|---|---|
| attention filter | Looks for the relation capable of organizing miscellaneous factual material, while treating each new project as a new structural problem (`J1`, `J2`). | Ask which relation makes these facts accumulate rather than merely coexist. |
| generative unit | Works with coded factual components whose placement may be anchored or movable (`J3`). | Create evidence units with fact, source, structural freedom, and possible relations. |
| development engine | Studies, separates, defines, codes, orders, drafts, and researches again when writing reveals a gap (`J3`, `J5`). | Alternate evidence mapping and drafting instead of pretending research and writing are sealed phases. |
| selection criterion | Keeps an ordering choice when it is factually lawful, creates attraction, and clarifies relations without changing the material (`J2`, `J4`). | Compare chronology, theme, encounter, place, and counterpoint against truth and reader movement. |
| uncertainty strategy | Records openly, distinguishes known from unverified, and returns to sources when composition exposes ignorance (`J5`). | Mark gaps as gaps; do not use smooth prose to conceal missing evidence. |
| audience model | Assumes readers need enough context to understand a reference without supplying the writer's missing description (`J6`). | Test every proper-name or cultural reference for carried versus borrowed vividness. |
| collaboration model | Treats subjects as people who entrust material to the writer and uses qualified review where technical accuracy requires it (`J5`). | Separate source verification from source control over interpretation; document which claims need expert checking. |
| correction loop | Reclassifies notes, reorders movable components, checks facts, trims without invention, and looks upstream when an ending fails (`J3`, `J5`, `J7`). | Revise structure, evidence, and ending together; do not polish a false order. |
| stopping rule | McPhee says he stops when he knows he cannot presently do better and may discover he finished earlier (`J7`). This is personal, not a portable objective rule. | Use the current brief's truth and acceptance gates; then ask whether any remaining change is demonstrably better. |
| characteristic risk | Structural craft can be caricatured as outlining before reporting or as a license to compress reality into a neat thesis (`J2`, `J5`). | Reject any elegant structure that depends on missing, renamed, selectively distorted, or contextless evidence. |

## Evidence-structure map

Use this instrument when the governing problem is source architecture.

| Field | Record |
|---|---|
| factual unit | the event, observation, quotation, object, claim, or consequence |
| provenance | source, date, confidence, and any verification needed |
| truth boundary | fact, supported inference, uncertainty, or forbidden invention |
| structural freedom | anchored, conditionally movable, or free-floating |
| anchor reason | chronology, causality, place, quotation context, or another constraint |
| relation candidates | theme, person, place, process, contrast, encounter, chronology, or other |
| reader job | what this unit lets the reader understand, feel, or question |
| reference burden | knowledge the reader must already possess; context required to repay it |
| omission risk | what would become false, unfair, or causally misleading if this unit disappeared |

After mapping, sketch at least two genuinely different lawful structures when the choice is consequential. A different opening with the same order is not a different structure.

## Story protocol

1. Inventory the current evidence before inventing connective language.
2. Mark each unit's truth boundary and structural freedom.
3. Name the current organizing candidates without treating chronology as automatic.
4. Arrange enough real material to test how each relation carries attention and causality.
5. Select one governing relation; name any secondary relation explicitly.
6. Draft until the prose exposes a missing fact, weak bridge, or borrowed reference; research or cut rather than bluff.
7. Check omission for fairness, context, and causal distortion.
8. If the ending strains, look upstream for the point where the movement already completed.

## Frame and sequence protocol

McPhee is not primarily a visual-style model. Use him when a visual essay, documentary sequence, information narrative, or exhibition has an evidence-order problem.

1. Treat each frame as an evidence unit with provenance and placement constraints.
2. Separate frames that must follow chronology or causality from frames that may move thematically.
3. Select an organizing relation before polishing layouts.
4. Test whether captions and references carry enough context at the point of encounter.
5. Remove a frame only after checking what factual or causal bridge it supplied.

## Questions

- What has actually been gathered, and what are we merely hoping is true?
- Which material is anchored by time, cause, place, or quotation context?
- Which person, process, place, question, or contrast connects otherwise separate material?
- Is chronology revealing the subject or merely reporting the order in which research happened?
- Which reference borrows vividness the audience may not possess?
- What becomes unfair or misleading if this section disappears?
- Did the work already end before the planned conclusion?

## Failure conditions

- An outline is chosen before enough evidence exists to challenge it.
- The structure turns a qualified fact into a clean universal claim.
- Chronology is broken at the cost of causal comprehension.
- A striking reference does the descriptive work instead of the writing.
- Omission erases a contradiction, affected person, or disconfirming fact.
- The dossier is invoked to make prose sound “New Yorker-like.”

## Abstain boundary

Abstain when the source is fictional, the factual architecture is already settled, the task is a local wording change, or another model's viewpoint, embodiment, visual relation, or sequence problem is the actual decision gap.
